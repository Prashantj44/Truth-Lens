# INTERNATIONAL MONETARY FUND & WORLD BANK: GLOBAL ECONOMIC OUTLOOK (2024 - 2026)
Source: International Monetary Fund (IMF) World Economic Outlook Database & Ministry of Finance Economic Survey
Publication Category: Government / Official International Financial Institution
Report Reference: IMF-WEO-2024-Q4 / WB-GEP-2025

SECTION 1: GLOBAL NOMINAL GDP RANKINGS (2024 - 2025)
According to the International Monetary Fund (IMF) World Economic Outlook database and World Bank national accounts data:
1. United States: $28.78 Trillion (Rank #1)
2. China: $18.53 Trillion (Rank #2)
3. Germany: $4.59 Trillion (Rank #3)
4. Japan: $4.11 Trillion (Rank #4)
5. India: $3.94 Trillion to $4.18 Trillion (Rank #5)
6. United Kingdom: $3.50 Trillion (Rank #6)
7. France: $3.13 Trillion (Rank #7)

SECTION 2: INDIA'S ECONOMIC GROWTH TRAJECTORY AND PROJECTIONS
India currently stands as the fifth-largest economy in nominal GDP terms globally as of 2024 and early 2025. While India is the fastest-growing major economy with real GDP growth averaging between 6.5% and 7.2%, India has NOT overtaken Germany or Japan to become the world's third-largest economy in 2025. 

Official projections from the IMF, S&P Global Ratings, and the Indian Ministry of Finance anticipate that India is on track to overtake Japan and Germany to reach the third position by 2027 or 2028, assuming sustained nominal growth rates of approximately 10-11% in domestic currency terms.

SECTION 3: PURCHASING POWER PARITY (PPP) DISTINCTION
In Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) terms, India has been the third-largest economy since 2011, behind only China and the United States, with a PPP GDP exceeding $13.5 Trillion. However, standard headline economic rankings refer strictly to nominal GDP measured in current US Dollars, where India remains in fifth place in 2025.

SECTION 4: INFLATION AND MONETARY POLICY
The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) maintained its benchmark repo rate at 6.50% throughout late 2024 before adopting a neutral monetary policy stance as headline retail inflation (CPI) moderated toward the 4% target band (+/- 2%). Foreign exchange reserves stood at historic highs above $680 Billion in late 2024.
