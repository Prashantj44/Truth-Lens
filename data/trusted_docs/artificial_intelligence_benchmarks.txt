# STANFORD INSTITUTE FOR HUMAN-CENTERED ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE (HAI) & IEEE
Source: Stanford AI Index Annual Report & IEEE Computer Society Technical Standards
Publication Category: Peer-Reviewed Academic Research Report
Report Reference: STANFORD-AI-INDEX-2024 / IEEE-CS-AI-771

SECTION 1: FRONTIER MODEL CAPABILITIES AND EVALUATION BENCHMARKS
The Stanford AI Index Report documents that state-of-the-art AI systems have surpassed human baseline performance on several standardized benchmarks, including image classification (ImageNet), English reading comprehension (SQuAD), visual reasoning (VQA), and grade-school mathematical problem solving (GSM8K).

However, current large language models continue to exhibit limitations on complex, multi-step symbolic reasoning, formal mathematical verification, and adversarial factuality benchmarks like TruthfulQA.

SECTION 2: COMPUTATIONAL REQUIREMENTS AND TRAINING COSTS
Frontier AI training compute has doubled roughly every 5-6 months since 2010, significantly outpacing classical Moore's Law (which observed semiconductor transistor density doubling every 18-24 months). Estimated training compute for leading frontier models exceeded 10^26 FLOPs in 2024, with estimated training cluster hardware and electricity expenditures exceeding $100 Million for individual foundation runs.

SECTION 3: HALLUCINATIONS AND RETRIEVAL-AUGMENTED GENERATION (RAG)
Generative AI language models are prone to hallucination when relying exclusively on parametric weights. Empirical studies in computational linguistics demonstrate that Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems incorporating dense vector embeddings, reranking stages, and strict context bounding reduce factual hallucination rates by over 70% to 85% compared to vanilla zero-shot prompt completion.
